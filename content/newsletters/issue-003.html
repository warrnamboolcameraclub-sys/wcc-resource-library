📷 Convention Corner: Ethical Bird Photography

Part 1: The Photograph Is Never More Important Than the Bird

As part of our ongoing Convention Corner series, we're exploring some of the ideas presented by acclaimed bird photographer Kim Wormald EFIAp Gold GMAPS SSVAPS during this year's VAPS Convention.

One of the strongest messages from Kim's presentation was surprisingly simple:

The welfare of the bird must always come before the photograph.

It sounds obvious, yet in the pursuit of a better image it can be easy to forget.

Kim encouraged photographers to constantly ask themselves a simple question:

"Is my presence changing the bird's behaviour?"

If the answer is yes, it may be time to step back.

Birds will often tell us when they are uncomfortable. Repeated alarm calls, changes in feeding behaviour, agitated movements or attempts to move away are all signs that we may be getting too close.

The challenge for wildlife photographers is learning to recognise these signals and respond appropriately.

Often the best solution is patience.

Rather than moving closer, wait.

Rather than forcing the image, observe.

Many of the most rewarding wildlife photographs occur when the bird becomes comfortable with your presence and resumes its natural behaviour.

A technically perfect image is never worth causing unnecessary stress to the subject.

As Kim reminded us, ethical bird photography is not about what we can photograph.

It is about what we should photograph.

📸 This Week's Challenge

Spend ten minutes simply observing a bird before taking a photograph.

Watch its behaviour.

Watch how it interacts with its environment.

Ask yourself whether your presence is influencing what it is doing.

You may be surprised how much more you see when the camera stays down for a few moments.

Many members enjoyed Ian van der Wolde's presentation on Long Exposure Photography at this year's VAPS Convention, and we know there is considerable interest in continuing that series.

Over the past two editions we have focused on other timely club matters and introduced our new Ethical Bird Photography series inspired by Kim Wormald's presentation at the Convention.

The good news is that the Long Exposure series is far from finished.

Over the coming months we intend to alternate between the two series, allowing members to explore a wider range of topics and ideas presented at VAPS.

Next week we return to Ian's presentation with:

Long Exposure Photography – Part 2: Seeing Differently – The Long Exposure Mindset

We'll explore why long exposure photography is often less about shutter speed and more about learning to slow down, observe carefully and pre-visualise the final image before pressing the shutter.

Future articles will cover shutter speed selection, ND filters, composition, creative techniques and practical ideas that can be explored close to home.
