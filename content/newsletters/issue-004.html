📷 Convention Corner

Long Exposure Photography – Part 2: Seeing Differently – The Long Exposure Mindset

Last month we introduced the first article in our series inspired by Ian van der Wolde's presentation at the VAPS Convention. This week we return to that series after introducing Ethical Bird Photography in our previous edition.

One of Ian's strongest messages was that long exposure photography isn't really about long shutter speeds at all.

It's about learning to slow down.

In an age where we're encouraged to shoot quickly and often, Ian challenged photographers to spend more time observing before pressing the shutter.

Rather than immediately setting up the tripod, ask yourself:

  • What first attracted me to this scene?
  • What is moving?
  • What isn't moving?
  • Would a longer exposure simplify the composition or add unnecessary blur?
  • What mood am I trying to create?

Long exposure photography encourages us to pre-visualise the final image before taking it.

Sometimes that means using a 30-second exposure to transform rough water into a smooth, ethereal surface.

Other times it might mean deciding that the movement is actually an important part of the story and using a much faster shutter speed instead.

The lesson applies well beyond landscapes.

Whether photographing wildlife, architecture, people or street scenes, taking a moment to slow down and think about the image before pressing the shutter often produces stronger photographs.

Long exposure is simply one tool that teaches us the value of patience.

📸 This Week's Camera Challenge

Visit a location you've photographed before.

Before taking a single image, spend at least five minutes simply observing.

Look for changing light, movement, reflections, patterns and compositions that you may have overlooked on previous visits.

Then ask yourself:

"How would I photograph this differently today?"
