📸 Photographer's Tips – Set 17

🌱 Beginner Tip – B017

Use Your Camera's Self-Timer for Sharper Photos

Your camera's self-timer isn't just for taking group photographs. It can also help you capture sharper images.

Even when your camera is mounted on a tripod, pressing the shutter button can introduce a small amount of movement, particularly during longer exposures.

Setting a 2-second self-timer allows the camera to settle before the photograph is taken.

This is especially useful for landscapes, night photography, macro photography and other situations where camera movement can affect sharpness.

Next time you're using a tripod, try taking two photographs: one using the shutter button normally and another using the self-timer. Zoom in and compare the results.

📷 Intermediate Tip – I017

Photograph Through Glass Without Reflections

Photographing through windows can be frustrating when unwanted reflections appear across your image.

Whether you're photographing through a shop window, a train window or an observation deck, reflections from behind the camera can compete with your subject.

One simple solution is to move your lens as close to the glass as possible, without touching it, and use your hand or a dark cloth to block surrounding light.

Avoid using flash, as it will often create a bright reflection in the glass.

Also check that your camera is focusing on the subject beyond the window rather than on marks or reflections on the glass itself.

A small change in position or angle can make a significant difference.

⭐ Advanced Tip – A017

Use Focus Stacking for Greater Sharpness

Sometimes even a small aperture cannot keep everything in your photograph acceptably sharp.

This is particularly common in macro photography, where depth of field becomes extremely shallow, or in landscapes with objects very close to the camera.

Focus stacking involves taking several photographs of the same scene, each focused at a slightly different distance.

Specialised software, or applications such as Photoshop, can then combine the sharpest areas from each image into a single photograph with greater apparent depth of field.

For best results, keep your camera stationary, use consistent exposure settings and work with subjects that aren't moving.

Some modern cameras even offer automatic focus bracketing to simplify the process.

The result can be a photograph with front-to-back sharpness that would be difficult or impossible to achieve in a single exposure.
