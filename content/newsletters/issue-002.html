🌑 Looking Ahead: September Set Subject – Low Key

Although September may seem a long way off, entries for the September competition will be submitted at our August meeting, making now the perfect time to start thinking about potential images.

Many members may already have a head start.

Our recent High Key and Low Key workshop produced some fantastic images and may have given you photographs that are worth revisiting with fresh eyes. Before heading out to create something new, take some time to review the images you captured during that evening. You might already have an entry sitting in your catalogue waiting to be rediscovered.

Low Key photography isn't simply about underexposing an image. It's about using darkness deliberately to create mood, mystery and impact.

Think about:

  • Subjects that suit dramatic lighting.
  • Using shadows to simplify a composition.
  • Isolating your subject against a dark background.
  • The emotions that darkness can convey.

📸 This Month's Challenge

Revisit the images from our recent High Key and Low Key workshop.

📷 Coming Soon: Ethical Bird Photography

Series Introduction: Inspired by Kim Wormald

Over the coming weeks we'll be exploring some of the ideas presented by acclaimed bird photographer Kim Wormald EFIAp Gold GMAPS SSVAPS, who recently spoke about the topic of ethical bird photography.

Kim's presentation challenged us to think beyond sharp images and dramatic moments, asking an important question:

"How do we create powerful wildlife photographs while ensuring the welfare of our subjects?"

Drawing on decades of experience photographing birds throughout Australia, Kim shared practical advice, personal stories, and examples that reminded us that patience and respect often lead to our strongest images.

Whether you photograph birds every weekend or simply enjoy the occasional encounter with wildlife, we hope this short series encourages all of us to think more deeply about the way we work in the field.

After all, no photograph is worth compromising the wellbeing of the creatures we love to photograph.

🕊️ When Photographs Become a Voice

One of the most inspiring aspects of Kim Wormald's work is how she uses photography beyond competitions and exhibitions.

Her images have helped support conservation campaigns, raise awareness of threatened species, document wildlife recovery after bushfires and contribute valuable records to researchers and community groups.

A photograph can do more than decorate a wall.

It can educate.

It can inspire empathy.

It can encourage action.

As photographers, we have the privilege of helping others see the natural world through our eyes.

Used thoughtfully, our images become more than art.

They become advocacy.

Perhaps that is the highest purpose wildlife photography can serve.

Our educational series inspired by this year's VAPS Convention continues over the coming weeks.

Many members enjoyed Ian van der Wolde's presentation on long exposure photography and there is still plenty more to explore. Upcoming articles will cover topics including:
